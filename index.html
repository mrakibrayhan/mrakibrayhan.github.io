01 — ABOUT

Designing hardware where machine learning will eventually run.

I'm an undergraduate student in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering (ECE) at North South University, majoring in Computer Science and Engineering (CSE), focused on digital systems and computer architecture. My work centers on Field-Programmable Gate Arrays (FPGAs) — building processors as real, synthesizable hardware and verifying them against the behavior they're meant to implement, rather than stopping at simulation.

Alongside RTL design and Electronic Design Automation (EDA) workflows, I'm increasingly interested in Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning — specifically how learned models eventually have to run on real, resource-constrained hardware, and what that implies for the architectures underneath them. My thesis work on a multi-cycle processor, and its extension into a pipelined design, is the foundation for that direction.

I like problems where a design either works on the board or it doesn't — there's no ambiguity once it's on silicon.
